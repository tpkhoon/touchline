## At a glance

Four prototype builds, Alpha 1, the polish and small-features backlogs and mobile readiness step 2 are done, and so are playtest feedback batches 1–6 and the rounds after them, up to round 24 (the newest: bulk edits and an On/Off switch for the in-game editor, a youth rating for every nation, sim forward, expansion drafts, loans and prospect management, and names of their own for more than thirty nations). Batch 7 (platform) is next, with a round 8 of ideas from comparative review merged in (batch 8 below), then Alpha 2 (editor and history) and a living-world backlog (with identity items from an external review: sandbox start, club recruitment philosophies, reputation web); device builds and a native store release follow. Phases are ordered but not yet dated.

## Shipped

The web prototype runs on a phone browser with no build step, covering 1877 fictional clubs in 132 leagues (the real structure under invented names) across 117 nations in three simulation tiers, and 117 national teams. Newest build first.

### Playtest feedback, round 24

- **Editing the world you play:** Settings → Edit the world has an On/Off switch (off hides every Edit button), the single-player and single-club edits, and a **Bulk edit** sheet that changes many at once with a live count on its button. It covers players (everyone, free agents, a nation or a league; a position group and an age range; attributes, potential, contract length, wages, morale, healing injuries), clubs (reputation, budget, bank balance, ground capacity, academy, training and medical levels), league and cup prize money, and nations' youth ratings.
- **Youth rating by nation:** every nation has a youth rating (about 50 is average; shown with a ▲ or ▼ on the World ranking and the nation's page). It moves a little each season toward what the nation's team (coefficient), its clubs' academies and its young prospects are worth, and sets the quality of its clubs' academy intakes: a little more ability and potential, and more wonderkids, in strong youth nations. Every season's rating is kept: the nation's page shows its place in the world and a line of its run since the world began, and a scout's region list shows each part of the world's average (🌱 with ▲ or ▼).
- **Eight more split leagues:** Northern Ireland (33 games then six and six), Iceland and Finland (22, then single-round groups), Slovakia (22, then double-round groups), Israel and Cyprus (26, then a top six and a bottom eight), Romania (30, then a top six and a bottom ten with points halved) and Bulgaria (26, then single-round groups) now split after their regular season like Scotland and Belgium.
- **Types of player for scouts:** an assignment can ask for a type as well as a position: ball-playing or no-nonsense centre-back, sweeper keeper, inverted full-back, wing-back, anchor man, regista, ball-winner, box-to-box, playmaker, inverted winger, inside forward, target man, poacher, false nine or pressing forward (the position and the role he plays best); the assignment line and the Scouting hub name it.
- **More small ones:** a Confirm before skipping switch in Settings makes Next match ask first (the 📅 sheet always does; both can be stopped while they run); the season review's league list has the eight biggest leagues as tap-to-follow chips, so a new career is not left with an empty card; and the world editor's national team screen sets a nation's starting youth rating (15 to 95, empty to let the game work it out).
- **Small conveniences:** the 📅 sheet opens on what you chose last time (days or a target); a bulk edit can be undone with an Undo button while the Bulk edit sheet is still open (it puts back the players', clubs', prize and youth-rating values it changed); and a queued expansion club has a Try another club button that swaps it for the next one waiting in the pool (the changes made to the old one go with it, and no two slots share a club).
- **Editor, sanctions and a balance check:** the world editor's International screen has Sanctions: a nation or a club kept out of international competition for some seasons (a ban or a what-if world). A nation sits out qualifying, the Nations League, the invitationals and the tournaments while it lasts; a club is passed over for its league's continental places, which go to the next club; the feed says when a ban begins and ends. The editor's home also has a Balance check, a look at the world before you play it: a club far ahead of its league, a league with no spread in reputation, a lower division stronger than the one above, each row opening the league.
- **Editing a nation itself:** the national team's screen in the world editor also edits the nation: its name, flag, part of the world, how its players play and how common they are in leagues abroad (a multiplier), saved with the world and put back when the database is cleared; the nations list marks a nation that was changed.
- **A world in CSV files:** the world editor has a CSV screen: this world's leagues, clubs and players as leagues.csv, clubs.csv and players.csv to open in a spreadsheet, templates to fill in, and an upload that puts the rows back (a club is matched by its id or its name, a column left empty changes nothing, a club with remove set is taken out, a player's attributes are made from his ability and position). Every problem is named by file, row and column and a row with a problem is left out while the rest go in. The same files start a world from Import a database on the new-career screen (a world put back from its own CSV is the same world).
- **When clubs fail, and phoenix clubs:** a club whose debts pass the limit at the start of a season goes into administration (a points deduction, 6 by default, and now a transfer embargo for the season: it cannot sign players), as before; and if the world allows it, a club that fails again within four years is liquidated: its players become free agents, the division below promotes its best club to fill the place, and so on down the pyramid, and supporters form a phoenix club (AFC and the old name, the same ground and colours, a small club owned by its fans) in the place left at the bottom. A club you manage, one in a closed league or with a B team, and clubs the world makes proof are never liquidated. The world editor's When clubs fail screen sets all of it (clubs can fail, embargo, liquidation, phoenix, the deduction, rich owners safe, and clubs that can never fail). The headlines of an administration, a liquidation and a ban stay in the feed (the news trimmer keeps up to 60 such rare stories, marked keep, instead of letting a day of transfer news push them out).
- **Engineering:** `npm run typecheck` (TypeScript over the files marked `// @ts-check`, twenty modules so far, now part of the deploy); `ui-live.js` and `ui-help.js` split out of the two largest screen files.
- **The text match view is the default:** matches play as live commentary (the same simulation, lighter on the battery) and the pitch view is switched off, with its Pitch / Text switch hidden in Settings; all of the pitch code stays in `js/matchview.js`, and `FM.MatchView.VISUAL = true` brings it back.
- **Setting up a career:** the first new-career screen has an Editing the world in game On/Off switch (Settings → Edit the world can still change it later), so a purist career never has Edit buttons.
- **Season review:** the end-of-season sheet lists your own league and only the leagues you follow (a Leagues you follow card with a hint to follow one), and cups only when followed or won by you, not the whole world's champions.
- **Simming:** the 📅 sheet now picks first and shows what it will do ("Sim 14 days", "Sim until your next match"), and nothing starts until Start simming is pressed; while a sim forward or a skip to the next match runs, a Stop simming button ends it at the end of the day it is on and keeps the days played.
- **Customising expansion clubs:** each queued expansion club on the Expansion card has an edit sheet for its name, short name, nickname, town, ground, capacity, reputation, colours and identity (checked for rude words and duplicate names), with a way back to the original; the changes are used when the club joins.
- **Sim forward to a target:** the 📅 sheet also has Until the window changes, Until my next match (stops on its day, before it is played) and Until the season ends (goes on through windows and deadline day; any of them still stops for a decision, a job offer or a sacking).
- **World editor:** a draft is saved (💾 button, autosave while you edit, a Continue card when you reopen it); a nation can be created (name, three-letter code, flag, part of the world, the culture its players' names come from, style, colours and how common its players are abroad), and with sixteen players it fields a national team.
- **Sim forward:** a 📅 button beside Advance and Next match asks how many days (3, 7, 14, 30, 60 or your own, up to 120) and plays them, your own matches as instant results. It stops early for a decision in the feed, a window opening or closing, deadline day, the season's end, a job offer or a sacking. (It had been squeezed out of sight on the home screen; it shows again.)
- **Expansion in closed leagues:** a league with no promotion or relegation shows an Expansion card on its table screen: add one to three clubs at the start of next season. 424 ready-made fictional clubs (js/expansion.js, tools/expansionpool.mjs) wait in the 106 closed leagues, and each fills its squad in an expansion draft (every club protects its best twelve and loses one to four players, replaced with younger ones). Split leagues (Scotland, Belgium, Czechia ...) refit their groups to the new size, and leagues of two zones or conferences (Argentina, the US) only take a number that keeps them equal. The card also has a No draft: start empty choice: the new clubs then start with no players at all, sign free agents and young players through the pre-season (about twenty by the first match), and no player is taken from the existing clubs.
- **Loans and prospects:** a new world starts with lower-division clubs holding about 400 players on loan from better clubs (two or three a club in the second and third divisions, mostly 18 to 22-year-olds). AI clubs manage their wonderkids and key prospects: one close to the first eleven is picked ahead of a slightly better player until he has played his share, and one too far from the team is loaned to a club where he would play, in every window. Your own loanees get a goal (appearances, and goals for a forward) and the feed reports every four games whether the loan is on track, with the recall offered when it is behind.
- **Scouting:** an assignment can target one exact position (ST, LW, RW, AM, LM, RM, CM, DM, LWB, RWB, LB, RB, CB) as well as a group; the three scouts a new manager starts with are one from the club's own nation and two from its part of the world (a Uruguayan club gets South Americans); staff on offer come mostly from the club's own part of the world and the regions it has ties with; reports discount the numbers of a player from a weaker league (down to 55%, with a note) and say what he will expect here (a key player, a starting place, rotation, development or back-up); a saved search can have a bell, and the feed names any player who newly matches it.
- **Dressing room:** a promise with a deadline can be renegotiated once with 🔁; the captain steps in first when a player's playing time lets him down (a strong leader settles far more of them, and a failed word opens the usual meeting); players remember what you told them, and meetings and promises quote it back ("You told me in October I was part of your plans."). Captains count: each season's end notes who wore the armband at every club, the legends card lists the longest-serving captains, and the feed tells when one of three years or more leaves your club.
- **Match and press:** a press-conference answer says what it did in brackets ("Fans like it. (squad morale +2, fans +3, board −1)"); pressing high on a short week (three days or fewer) costs 25% more legs and tired legs make mistakes late on.
- **Settings:** the default currency is pounds (Auto still follows the club's country); a Reduced motion switch; a Colour-blind kits switch (the away shirt differs to red-green colour blindness too, and the away side's players are squares on the pitch).
- **Realistic histories:** a player's earlier seasons follow his rank in his position at the club, so a star started most matches (last season's rating around 6.8 to 7.2) and a fringe player played little.
- **Choosing a club:** the browse screen has a dropdown for each continent holding its nations and their leagues, the question screen's Where? list is arranged by continent, and the world ranking on the International screen folds away behind its title with a part-of-the-world filter.
- **Names of nations and clubs:** thirty-one nations named from a neighbour's library now have words of their own (tools/namelib-new.mjs): Slovakia, Slovenia, Albania, Kosovo, Bulgaria, Moldova, Finland, Iceland, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Georgia, Armenia, Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Jamaica, Trinidad and Tobago, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Jordan and Iraq (towns by each language's endings, clubs, nicknames, colours, divisions and cups in the nation's own words). Romania, Sweden, Algeria, Tunisia, Malaysia, Singapore, China, Canada, New Zealand, Egypt and the Gulf got the same, and about 530 clubs were renamed (tools/renameclubs.mjs; codes, colours, reputations and founding years unchanged). Sixteen nations (and Sweden and Norway) have player name pools of their own. About 250 more club nicknames (colour nicknames are rarer; tools/renick.mjs).
- **Lore consistency:** record crowds from the 1990s on stay within the ground; a rivalry that says two clubs split from one starts in the year the younger was founded; a police, army, union, railway or works club shows as State-Backed, Society-Owned or Company-Backed (new owners, also in the editor); anthem notes have 24 variants and a big club with no title still has an era; the generator refuses 450 more famous footballers' names in either order and any accent (hashes only, tools/realnames.mjs); abbreviations are unique and never rude; players have names from one community (India, New Zealand, Jordan); Vietnamese players have a middle name and a given name and are known by the given name.
- **Removed:** the English county cups (the 14 Senior Cups; older saves drop them), and the map (the By region start and the league screen's Map tab; distances and away trips stay).
- **Saves:** the Load a save screen has a rename button (a name of your own, kept through later saves and shown on the title screen's Continue button) and a delete button (a second tap confirms).

### Playtest feedback, round 23

- Super cups: one match at a neutral ground early in each season, 122 of them. In every nation the league champions meet the cup winners (the league's runner-up when one club did both); on each continent the champions cup winner meets the winner of its Shield (the European, South American, African, Asian and North American Super Cups). They have their own day after the first league round, a card on the Cups screen, count as a trophy for the winner and are kept apart from the season's cups in the archive. A new world has none in its first season (there is no last season).
- Six more regional club cups, groups of four then a final like the other regional cups: the Arab Club Cup (eight Arab leagues, taking the places after the Gulf Club Cup's), the West African Club Cup, the East and Southern African Club Cup, the Central American Club Cup, the Andean Club Cup and the Atlantic Club Cup (Scotland, Wales, Ireland and Northern Ireland). 22 continental cups in all, none of them with a club in two of them.
- Fifteen more regional tournaments for nations: the East African, Southern African, West African, North African, Arab, Central Asian, Nordic, Central European, Celtic, Caucasus, Central American and Caribbean, Andean and River Plate cups, the Baltic Cup and the Balkan Cup join the Southeast Asian, Gulf and East Asian ones and the three older invitationals: a host that rotates through the region's nations and the three best-ranked others. Three are played each season in turn (no nation in two of them, and none in a season where the host or guests have qualifiers in the window), so each comes round about every seven seasons.

### Playtest feedback, round 19

- English club names: Alexandra, Wednesday, Orient and Argyle are one-offs, as in real life (the generator keeps at most one of each in the world, and two Harriers); Redworth Alexandra, New Lamwold Wednesday, North Yeothorpe Alexandra and Old Yeowick Alexandra became Redworth Athletic, New Lamwold United, North Yeothorpe Albion and Old Yeowick City.
- The Intercontinental Cup, every season, mid-season: the continental champions of the season before, with the Asian and African champions meeting first (the African–Asian Cup), the American champions meeting in the Derby of the Americas, the winners playing the Challenger Cup and the European champion joining them in the final. Single matches at neutral grounds.
- The new World Club Championship format, in the summer of every fourth season (the year before the World Cup): 32 clubs in eight groups of four (a single round robin), then a round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals and the final. Places by continent (Europe 12, South America 6, Asia 5, Africa 4, North America 5): the continental champions of the last four seasons first, then by continental points (winners 10, runners-up 7, semi-finalists 5, quarter-finalists 3, a group stage 1, kept four seasons), no more than two clubs of a nation where there is a choice. The draw uses four pots and keeps a continent out of a group (Europe may have two). It has its own calendar days after the playoffs, a tab of the Cups screen with group tables and its history, and a prize fund. Older saves get the Intercontinental Cup and the new format from their next season.
- A map of the world (since removed; distances, away trips and the longest-trip note stay): `tools/geocode.mjs` gives every club a place from the real slot it sits on (the city's coordinates, which are plain facts; Wikipedia and Wikidata, with a small table of corrections), written to `js/geo.js`; the names stay fictional. Distances (`FM.Lore.km`) feed long away trips (a point of fitness for each 230 km beyond 350, up to 9, for the visitors, and a coach or charter cost for the away club, shown under Away travel in the finances; the longest trip of a league is noted on its table), and local derbies (neighbours within 30 km that had no rival of their own take each other, named for their towns, about 280 rivalries in all).
- Older glory: every club that existed before the generated seasons has a summary of the years before them: its league titles (years), its seasons in the top flight and its best decade, from its nation's first top-flight season (`FM.Lore.earlyHistory`; no table is stored). Each nation's champions are drawn year by year from how strong each club stood (a fallen giant had its best days at least twenty years before the generated seasons, an oil-backed club had none), no club wins more than a fifth of the seasons, and every Fallen Giant has at least three titles (so Aldcaster Wednesday, founded 1881, has its own). It shows as Honours before the records under every club's honours.
- Legends with variety: the plain lines (nine before, 60 now) and the stories drawn from a club's past (title, cup, staying up: 14, 10 and 11 wordings) are used least-used first within each nation, so no line is seen more than about a hundred times in a world of 1,000 clubs (the most common was 340); a save from before has its repeated lines rewritten once.
- Club details for every club (a card on every club's sheet): motto, anthem, mascot, ground nickname, record crowd, record win and heaviest defeat, most famous match (a cup final, title decider or promotion from the club's own generated past), named eras (the Golden Years, the Wilderness Years, the Cup Years, `The <legend's surname> Years`), and what kind of club it is.
- Rivalries with an origin: every derby has where it began (the title race, the cup final or the relegation both clubs shared in the generated seasons, the town they share, a feud) and the record between the two clubs before the save.
- Competition histories: each league, cup and continental competition has a History fold-out (founded, its trophy and the person who gave it, changes of format, the most titles, the giant-killings of the generated cup finals; the oldest cup in the world is marked), also on the Intercontinental and World Club Championships.
- Special club types are named and explained: military, police and security, railway, works and company, trade-union, university and reserve sides show a badge beside the club's identity and a paragraph on the club card ("Players serve here during national service" for Bujeong Garrison).
- The world's people: former players of the generated past are now in the game: about one club in ten has a legend of its own or of its rival as manager (named on the opposition card for the match, with a line when he faces his old club), one club in five has a legend as chairman or president, three in ten have an ambassador, and three great names of the big clubs join the Touchline Tonight panel. Legends' own cards say what they do now.

### Playtest feedback, round 18

- Proper restarts in the match view: after a goal and at half-time the sides walk back to their own halves, the side that did not score keeps outside the centre circle, and the ball is played from the centre spot (the second half is kicked off by the other side).
- A striker played in by a through ball or on a counter takes it toward goal before he shoots.
- A small shirt in each side's kit colour beside its name at the top of the match screen.
- Goal kicks and throw-ins on the pitch, with their share and landing places from the Wyscout data (`npm run carries`); a defensive wall for direct free kicks; free-kick crosses with the box filling as for a corner; carries fitted to the DFL open tracking (7 Bundesliga matches) and the PFF World Cup tracking (64 matches) at equal weight: about 34% of receptions are followed by a carry of 4 m or more, median 6.7 m (`npm run carries:tracking`), take-ons (about 26 a match, a win takes the carrier past, a loss gives the defender the ball) and short touches (1 to 4 m, after about half of receptions) from the real data. The StatsBomb open data (360 frames, `npm run layouts`) places the players at corners, throw-ins and goal kicks as real ones stand, sizes the free-kick wall by distance (6, 4.6, 2 and 1.2 men), and adds a third measure of carries and take-ons. The PFF World Cup events (`npm run events:pff`) set the goal kicks (about 13 a match) and throw-ins (about 38) of the match view and the pass accuracy of the match stats.
- Fewer unrealistic passes: no pass back to the keeper from the opponent's half, fewer passes back in that half, a pass into the box with no shot behind it is usually lost, and the ball is not passed around inside the box. Carriers past halfway dribble further and more often.
- The first Analyst view slice, in the post-match Shots & xG tab: the match in numbers, an xG timeline with each goal's scorer, minute and score, red cards and substitutions marked, a chart of who was threatening minute by minute, and each side's best shooters.
- The Analyst profile card on every player's sheet: per-90 percentiles against his position, a written summary, a shot map and the three players most like him; the engine records expected goals, expected assists and shot places for every player in every simulated match.
- The Analyst profile card on every club's sheet: its league season per match ranked among the league's clubs, a style radar, a written summary, the formations used, the run of its expected goals and (for your own club) where its chances come from.
- League charts: a Charts tab on the league screen with five scatter plots (chances for and against, goals against expected, possession against chance quality, pressing against possession, and every scorer's goals against expected goals), your club ringed and a tap opening any club or player.
- Tools to build a pack of your own: `npm run realplayers:fetch` and `realplayers:build` read openly licensed squads (Wikipedia, Wikidata) and write a world pack in the game's own format, with ratings estimated from how well known each player is, to a private folder outside the repository (`tools/private.mjs`). The game ships no pack and no real names.

### Playtest feedback, round 17

- The Nations League replaces the international friendlies: each confederation plays one in the seasons with no qualifying (the year with no tournament and the year of the Continental Champions Trophy), in leagues of sixteen nations and groups of four, on the first three matchdays of the breaks. Group winners go up, the last in each group go down, and the best League A group winner is champion. A nation with nothing to play in a window rests.
- Confederations in the world editor: International football → Confederations lists the five and any you add, and moves a nation between them. A confederation sets qualifying, the Nations League and the continental championship; one with four nations or more gets a World Championship place and a championship of its own.
- Prize money for leagues, cups and competitions: a league pays by final position from a fund the editor can set (automatic is a part of each club's revenue), a domestic cup pays its fund down the rounds, a continental or world cup pays per win and for the title; the Finances tab lists what the club won, the cups and league screens show the funds.
- A currency picker in Settings with 38 currencies at fixed rates (Auto follows the club's country).
- Catalonia and the Basque Country can be added as nations (new-career screen and the world editor): players of Catalan or Basque family can choose them, and each fields a national team with its own flag.
- Dropping down in the editor: a continental cup can send the clubs that finish third in a group into the cup one tier below, to play its group runners-up in a knockout play-off before the quarter-finals (tier 1 into tier 2, tier 2 into tier 3).

### Steady scoring pass

- Fixed goals per match creeping from ~2.6 to ~3.0 over 10 seasons; now flat (trend −0.01 to 0.00 a season, every season 2.6–2.9).
- Two root causes: players who developed in the simulation barely grew their secondary attributes, so defending eroded as academy products replaced generated players; and new managers picked tactics at random instead of the usual mix, drifting the world toward open shapes.
- Plus tactical equilibrium: if the whole game drifts, the chance rate eases back toward the calibrated level each summer (capped ±15%, never changing differences between teams).
- Academy and generated-player potential retuned so each generation's elite is closer to the last (elite growth down from +0.5 to about +0.3 ability a season).
- Calibration adds goals-trend, elite-trend and player-shape measures

## Open questions and risks

- **Licensing:** club, league and competition names are trademarks, so the game ships fictional leagues, clubs and competitions with fictional players (known real name combinations are blocked). Real names are left to packs people build themselves with the tools, kept out of the repository and the game's site. Decision (round 8): the game ships fictional; the names database and any pack live in a private folder.
- **Tech stack for release:** proposed answer — keep the web engine inside a native wrapper (Capacitor) rather than porting; the game is plain HTML/CSS/JS with no server.
- **Store updates:** JavaScript updates still normally go through store review, so fixes can't be pushed instantly.
- **Performance at scale:** the 30-league world has about 11,300 players; a league day takes ~0.7–1.35 s and pre-season days ~0.15–0.2 s in Node, and saves are ~7 MB with the compact player format. Save size is not a constraint (the target is a downloadable app with native file storage); the 5 MB localStorage fallback only matters for the web prototype in private browsing. Mid-range phones are still untested (content gate).
- **Balance:** after the training-ground cap, calibration is 49/50 in range on a 3-season run (only miss: champion was a top-3 club, 67% against 70–90%; goals per match 2.77–2.81, over-30s' minutes 20–23%) and the 2-season `npm test` passes.
  - The 10-season wonderkid test (348 followed) is 8/8 in range: 23% reach world class, 59% become good professionals, 18% never pass 69, 6% flop outright, median peak age 26.
  - Earlier: calibration was 50–51/51 on two seeds (title dominance, elite growth, injuries, retirement ages and upsets in range; over-30s' share of top-flight minutes ends six seasons at 24–25%, real 17–28, though it still rises from ~19% early on); one seed still shows the elite improving a little too fast.
  - Finances and difficulty still need tuning from playtests.
- **Scope:** the editor and history modes are large; they may need to ship after 1.0.
