## At a glance

Four prototype builds, Alpha 1, the polish and small-features backlogs and mobile readiness step 2 are done, and so are playtest feedback batches 1–6 and the rounds after them, up to round 24 (the newest: bulk edits and an On/Off switch for the in-game editor, a youth rating for every nation, sim forward, expansion drafts, loans and prospect management, and names of their own for more than thirty nations). Batch 7 (platform) is next, with a round 8 of ideas from comparative review merged in (batch 8 below), then Alpha 2 (editor and history) and a living-world backlog (with identity items from an external review: sandbox start, club recruitment philosophies, reputation web); device builds and a native store release follow. Phases are ordered but not yet dated.

## Shipped

The web prototype runs on a phone browser with no build step, covering 3711 fictional clubs in 253 leagues (the real structure under invented names) across 117 nations in three simulation tiers, and 117 national teams. Newest build first.

### Playtest feedback, round 24

- **Loans on the squad screen, first club, editor confirmation:** (1) a player on loan at your club has "on loan from {club}" on his line in the squad list (as well as the "Loan · club" pill), and your own players out on loan are listed under "Out on loan" on the squad screen, with the club they are at. (2) A player's first club is the club that trained him: the seasons at 17 to 19 of a player made with the world are at his youth club (`p.youth` or `p.trainedAt`), with the first spell and the rows to match; older saves have his first seasons moved there, once, when his profile is opened (`W.reworkPast`, history version 2). (3) Applying changes in the world editor, for a new career or a career in progress, ends with a sheet that stays until closed: how many changes were made and the first twelve of them.
- **Intercontinental play-offs, sub-confederations, Slavic and Balkan cups:** (1) *Play-offs:* the last places at the World Championship are played for as in real life: the best nation of each part of the world that did not qualify (Europe settles its own in its pools' play-offs), a few more of the next best to make three for each place, the top seeds waiting and the rest playing semi-finals in the third break, each winner meeting a seed in the final on the second day, single matches on neutral ground (`Intl.icpoDay`); the winners are in the finals, and the Nations tab shows the bracket. A calendar without a third break fills the places by ranking as before. (2) *Sub-confederations:* 19 unions within or across the confederations (Nordic, Baltic, Balkan, Slavic, Central European, Celtic, Caucasus, North, West, East and Central, and Southern African, the Southeast, East, West (Gulf) and Central and South Asian, the Central American and Caribbean, Andean and River Plate, and the Arab Football Union), each with a name, a parent confederation and members that can overlap, and each holding the Nations Cup drawn from its members; the world editor's Confederations screen renames them, changes their confederation, moves nations in and out and adds sub-confederations of its own (`Intl.subs`, `S.confeds.subs`). (3) *Slavic and Balkan cups:* a Slavic Nations Cup (13 nations) beside the Balkan one (now "Balkan Nations Cup"), and a Balkan Club Cup and a Slavic Club Cup, tier-5 regional cups for the clubs of those nations that take the places below the other regional cups' in each league. The game now has 24 continental cups.
- **Realistic past seasons:** the seasons a player had before the world began (`W.pastSeason`) are now drawn the way a season goes: the games he was fit for (injuries by his own proneness and age: mostly a few missed games, one season in four or five a real spell out), his place in the team, minutes from starts and substitute appearances (a keeper plays all or none of a match), then goals, assists, shots, key passes, tackles, interceptions and cards per 90 minutes for his position at the match engine's own rates (measured over a played season of the whole world: a regular striker 0.42 goals a game, a winger 0.22, a midfielder 0.13, a centre-back 0.08), raised or lowered by how far above his club's level he was, his finishing or vision, and a run of good or bad form that carries from season to season. Counts are drawn by chance, so a striker's 17 goals one year are 9 the next; ratings run from 6.6 to 7.3 with the player's quality, and a keeper's clean sheets and goals against follow his side's strength. A save made with the old numbers (which ran high for attackers, with the same minutes every year and no injuries) has them drawn again, once, the first time the player is looked at; his spells and career totals move by the difference. New worlds write up to 24 seasons. A unit test holds the rates.
- **Days counted as days:** Sim forward by a number of days now goes on for exactly that many days of the calendar, never past it: a day with no match, cup night or break just goes by and the date in the top bar moves on by one (`S.dnNow`, `Season.dnToday`, `simDaysSteps`); "Stopped after N days" says how many days really went by. The Until choices (next match, window, season) and Continue still go from one day with something on to the next, and the daily running of the world (training, healing, transfers) still happens on the days with something on, not on the quiet ones.
- **Loan indicator, optional substitute keeper:** a player on loan at your club shows a "Loan · club" pill in the squad list (hover or long-press for the full name and the end of the loan). A bench you set yourself no longer gets a substitute keeper added: the game picks one only when you leave the bench to it (`W.pickXI`), and if the keeper has to go off with none on the bench an outfield player takes the gloves (`Match.makeSub`).
- **A full season-by-season history:** a player's Season by season table has a row for every season since he was 16, with no "Earlier" or "Before: other clubs" lines. The seasons the game recorded show as before; any other is rebuilt from what is known (`W.fullHistory`): the club of the spell it falls in, or his youth club or a club of his nation at his level before his first spell, with the games and goals his spells and career totals have over the recorded rows shared out by his age then (and an estimate for early seasons an older save left out of the totals). A rebuilt row is dimmed with a ~ and shows games and goals only; it is worked out from his id and the year, so it is the same every time, and the Career total follows the table. New worlds now write up to 24 past seasons instead of 16.
- **Fold all, profile tabs, registration at the deadline, one budget slider:** (1) the Nations tab (tournaments, qualifying pools, Nations League) and the World leagues view each have Open all and Fold all (`UI.foldBar`, `foldAll`, `wvFoldAll`), beside the one the Cups screen already had. (2) Switching tabs in a player profile (overview, attributes, career ...) keeps the tab strip where it was instead of scrolling to the top. (3) The manager's signings are no longer refused for breaking the squad limits (the squad list, non-EU and foreign places, the matchday cap): the offer sheet warns, the deal goes through, and the squad is registered within the rules at the registration deadline (the players over a limit sit out until the next window closes). The club's own signing policy, embargo, work permit, signings from abroad a league allows and MLS salary rules still refuse (`Reg.canSign` with `soft`). (4) The wage and transfer budgets are one slider that moves the club's money from wages to transfers.
- **Less memory after a sim (music stopping):** the game has no audio code, so a player's music stopping when a sim finished was put down to memory: a big world is hundreds of megabytes as live objects, and the simulation worker kept a whole second copy of it for as long as the page was open. The worker now lets the world go as soon as the day has been sent back (the next run sends a fresh copy), which takes roughly half the memory the page holds when idle. Not confirmed on the phone that reported it.
- **A third international break:** the season has three windows of two match days, after league rounds 7, 15 and 20 (the last late in the season, as the June window). The first two carry the qualifiers and the Nations League as before; the third has every nation without a match play a friendly against a neighbour in the ranking (your own nation rests unless you set up a friendly), and the season's invitational tournaments (semi-finals and final) now play there, where no qualifier or Nations League game can get in the way. The world editor's calendar has a third break to move; a definition with two breaks keeps two, with the invitationals on the second (`D.INTL_AFTER`, `Intl.breakDay`).
- **Family ties for nations of your own:** a nation made in the world editor can name the nation its people have family in (Greenland: Denmark; the territory presets fill it in) and the share of its players with those ties (40% unless set): they are generated with names of that culture and a second nationality, and the usual citizenship rules settle whether they hold the passport. A small share of the parent's own players have a link back. A completely new country with no ties gets about 8% of its players with family in nations of its part of the world (`familyTies` in `js/worlddef.js`). Before, a nation of your own had only players of its own culture and no dual nationals.
- **Squad status filter, sliders, budget speed, better crests:** (1) the squad screen's filters have a Status choice: counts as foreign or non-EU, non-EU, EU passport, foreign Asian, ASEAN, African or American, homegrown, not homegrown, dual nationals (`SQ_REG`, by the league's own registration rules). (2) Contract and transfer negotiations use sliders for the fee, wage, signing-on, appearance and goal bonuses, release clause, wage rise and contract length, and for your asking fee when selling; the figure follows the thumb and the sheet redraws when it is let go. (3) The transfer and wage budgets are sliders too, and no longer save the whole game and redraw the screen on every tap: the figure updates at once and the save follows 1.2 seconds after the thumb stops (`Finance.setBudget`). (4) Club crests: eight shapes (hexagon, lozenge, oval, scalloped shield added), seventeen patterns (diagonal stripes, chequers, saltire, split, target, double chevron, chief added), a soft light, a dark or light edge by the base colour and a fine inner border on about two crests in three.
- **Hosts in qualifying (option):** each tournament in the world editor can have its hosts play the qualifying groups too (`tn.hostsQual`): they are drawn in and play every match but take no place, so the next team in their group goes through. Off by default: hosts skip qualifying, as checked for every tournament.
- **Seven requests, one batch:** (1) *Observer status:* in the world editor's Confederations screen each nation has an Observer switch; an observer belongs to its confederation and plays its Nations League and friendlies but is left out of the qualifying groups, the continental championship, the World Championship places and guest lists (`S.confeds.observers`, `Intl.observer`, `Intl.qualRanked`). (2) *More territories to turn on:* Create a nation offers sixteen more starting points (Galicia, Canary Islands, Brittany, Corsica, Sicily, Sardinia, Frisia, Sark, Falklands, Saint-Pierre, Wallis and Futuna, Zanzibar, Western Sahara, Tibet, Kurdistan, Northern Cyprus) beside the existing Catalonia, Basque Country, Jersey, Guernsey, Vatican City, Monaco and Greenland. (3) *A national team is a job:* a manager with a country and no club is "between clubs", not out of work (`W.hasNationJob`, `W.hasJob`): the Home screen, top bar and offers screen say so. (4) *Clubs come for you:* a bigger club may approach a manager who is doing better than the board expected (not often: 70 days in the job, 8 games played, one approach at a time, a week to answer, a cool-down after) with a card on Home; Accept leaves your club (a successor is appointed), Decline pleases the board and fans a little (`Season.poachTick`). (5) *Tournament hosts:* the editor lets each tournament have chosen hosts (up to four, for co-hosted finals) or draw one to four; hosts are in without qualifying whatever their ranking, head the pots and play at home against a team that is not a host. (6) *Short names:* `UI.cshort` and `Intl.tshort` give a short name for tight spaces (the Home table label, cup pills, the match hero); "Canadian Canadian Championship" is now "Canadian Championship". (7) *Press conferences:* 26 new questions (injuries, contracts, the window, form, the title race and relegation, the derby, youth, scorers, board money, boos, the papers, being linked with another club, foreign squads, rich clubs, international duty, draws).

- **Nationalities, the rest of the plan:** AI buying now leans toward the nationalities a league's mix is rich in (`T.natAffinity`) and back toward the league's starting foreign share (`S.natTarget`, `T.natBalance`); the foreign nations in a mix are flattened a little (`FOREIGN_FLATTEN`), so the big five average 10.7 nationalities a squad (was about 5); small nations with 16 to 22 players are topped up to a pool of 23 at world creation (`W.fillNationPools`, 78 nations under 23 became 36); a league's academies draw from its mix at a younger, more domestic share; and a fictional nation in the editor can stand in for a real one (`template`), taking its ties and its pull in every league. The first drift reading (10 seasons, every league at the quick tier): 108 of 117 top flights lost more than 5 points of foreign share, up to 43. With the academy change, 3 seasons move 30 of 117 by more than 5 (largest England -19, Germany -18, Turkey -16, France -15): better, not solved. The league targets are still rough and unchecked: no per-league CIES table could be found; the loans between lower-division clubs are domestic and need no corridor.

- **Youth pathway, first steps:** the Pathways card marks a youngster who is "ready to step up" (an Under-18 as good as the middle of the Under-21s, an Under-21 or B-team player good enough for the first team) and one who "needs more minutes" (a prospect playing under a quarter of his club's matches, or under a third out on loan); a minutes plan for each player (play regularly, bench cover, loan for minutes), warned against on the card (`UI.youthFlag`, `UI.PLANS`). Development now depends on the challenge of the football a young player plays (`Sea.challengeK`): about a level above his own teaches most, cruising far below teaches less and being far out of his depth hurts, the Under-21s and Under-18s count as a level or two below their club; the curve is set so the world's growth is as before (the regens check passes 10 of 12 as it did, the two near misses unchanged). A youth-pipeline check (`npm run calibrate:youth`, `tools/youth-check.mjs`) measures how many academy players reach a first team and a top flight, debut ages, the academies' share of top-flight games and whether loans help.

- **A job board for out-of-work managers, and Mauritius's names:** out of work (and with a national team only) the Home screen has a Job board under the offers: about a dozen clubs in your reputation range that are looking for a manager (the unhappy boards come first, refreshed every week), each with a Look and an Apply button and how good the chance is (a good chance, a fair chance, a long shot), and your open applications with the days to a reply (`Sea.jobBoard`, `Sea.applyForJob`, `UI.jobBoardHtml`). Three applications at once at most; each is answered in two to six days (`Sea.answerApplications`, run by the job market every day): an offer that stands for a week, or a no. The odds follow the gap between the club's reputation and yours (0.7 at five points above you, falling by 3.5 per point, up 0.15 where the board wants a change). Clubs of reputation 75 or more want a Continental B licence and 85 or more Continental A (`Sea.jobGate`), shown on the card with the Apply button disabled. Taking a job ends the applications and clears the board. Checked in Node (an out-of-work manager of reputation 38: a board of 12 clubs, a fourth application refused, all three answered with offers within nine days, a club of reputation 88 refusing a Continental B manager). Mauritius had every player named from a South Asian pool: its players now take the names of the island's communities (66% Indian descent, 27% Creole and French, 4% Chinese, 3% English; `comm('MRI')`). Not done: income for a manager with no club (the game has no personal money to pay it into), interviews with questions, minutes at Minimal fidelity, the half-time glitch, the world-size warning, the Rest fixtures' explanation.
- **Playtest report: save loading, national jobs and smaller fixes:** (1) A save that will not open shows a screen with the real reason and the step it failed in (reading, rebuilding the world, the world's history, upgrading, finishing the day, drawing the first screen), with Export this save and Delete this save (a second tap confirms), instead of a silent return to the title (`UI.loadFailed`; the console logs the step). (2) A skip that goes round without getting anywhere throws a reason (`Sea.simDaysSteps`, `Sea.skipSteps`) and the sim's toast shows it, in place of "Simulating the matchday" for ever. (3) A manager with a national team and no club has only that job: no skip, timed sim or "sim without stopping" plays a match of the nation's unseen; each stops before it (`natMatch`, with a toast). Checked in Node on Scotland, whose match is on day 40: the wait for an offer stopped there and no day was played with the match waiting. (4) Licences: club and national-team matches both count toward the games a licence needs, the Manager screen shows "3 of 20", and a manager with a national team and no club can enrol, the federation paying the fee (`Pe.gamesInCharge`, `Pe.startCourse`). (5) A national result moves a manager's reputation three times as much as before (a win was 0.6 of a point, invisible next to the 6 a trophy gives: now 1.8). (6) The Squad, Transfers and Scouting tabs of a manager with no club but a national team say where the national squad is and link to it (`UI.noClubView`). (7) The opposing national manager in the matchday dialog is the federation's (`t.mgr`), the same the Nations screen shows, not a club staff record a nation does not have. (8) A qualifying objective says "Win Africa C to qualify" where the pool has a group for each place, and not "9 places from Africa C" (the places of the whole pool) (`I.objective`). (9) "Coming up" counts days by the calendar (`dn`), as the sim does, not entries of it. (10) The advice about a player out of position offers Auto-pick XI when there is no natural to swap in. (11) A derby is "the Old Firm", not "the The Old Firm" (`U.theName`, three unit checks). Not done: a job board with applications, income for a manager with no club, a permanent test of offers over a season, what "Rest" fixtures are (dates, draw timing, stage), minutes at Minimal fidelity, the half-time glitch, the name pool for Mauritius, and the world-size warning that differed between two creations.
- **Your league and its neighbours are always Full, and league detail can be changed every season:** your league, the one above it and the one below it (where there is one) are Full for as long as you are at the club, whatever was chosen, and cannot be set lower (`W.focusLeagues`, `W.applySimFocus`: it used to leave a league chosen as Minimal alone). When the club changes league (promotion or relegation) the three follow it and the league it leaves goes back to the tier chosen at the start or the last time (a second-division club with a Minimal world: divisions 1, 2 and 3 are Full; relegated, 3, 2 and 4 are Full and division 1 is Minimal again), and when the job ends every league goes back to its chosen tier. A league that becomes Full gets the bigger squads of that tier (`W.topUpFullSquads` at the season rollover and when a job is taken). The tier of every league can be changed on the last day of each season, for the next one: a card on Home that day and a League detail screen in Settings (read-only the rest of the year) list the leagues by nation with Full, Light and Minimal for each, "everything else Minimal" and "everything else Light", and the leagues around your club shown as always Full; the choices wait in `S.simNext` and take effect when the season ends (`Sea.applySimNext`). A skip that reaches the last day stops once for it (`Sea.detailDue`, a pending decision), unless the sim runs on its own. Tools and tests that play with Minimal worlds now get the three leagues around their club in Full too. Checked in Node (promotion and relegation of a second-division club, the pending choice, the job ending, the stop) and in the browser (the card, the sheet with the leagues around the club locked, Everything else Light, Done, read-only off the last day).
- **Leagues you did not choose start as Minimal, and an out-of-work career gets job offers in any world:** the new-career default for a league the player does not choose is Minimal (it was Light; with the world at 4,800 clubs every league Light made a day of play cost about 35 CPU seconds in the transfer window; with the default Minimal it is 3.6 on average). Your own league and the ones just above and below it are still Full, and every league can still be set to any tier on the new-career screen, whose text now says so. Job offers (`Sea.jobMarket`) came only from Full leagues and two lower divisions in Light, so a career that began with no club (no league of its own to be Full) in a world of Minimal leagues had no job to offer, ever (a playtest report: nine months without an offer): any club of any tier can now have a vacancy, within the usual reputation window. The leagues around your club are Full whatever was chosen (next bullet), and the squads of the clubs that became Full are filled up once to the Full size (`W.topUpFullSquads`, marked per club). Tested in Node on an all-Minimal world: an out-of-work manager of reputation 38 got 3 offers at the start and 14 clubs offered over 40 days; he took a job in a Peruvian second division, whose league and neighbours went Full (34 clubs, 226 players made), and played on. Not done: a permanent test of offers over a season, the job board with applications, reputation gain from national-team results, and the other items of that report.
- **A day of play, profiled (a first 12% faster, the world unchanged):** a CPU profile of two days of a new career at 4,860 clubs put 88% of the time in the AI transfer window (`T.aiWindow`), which runs every day the window is open. Two exact fixes: the loan search for prospects (`T.aiProspects`) looked through every club of the world for every prospect and worked out each one's squad again each time (now the clubs are listed once and a squad is worked out once between two signings), and the market list worked a player's age out four times (now once). Three days of play from the same prepared world and seed give the same state hash before and after, at 32 to 46 CPU seconds a day instead of 39 to 51. Not done, and the real size of it: the window's work grows with the number of Full and Light clubs (the day's shoppers are 60 a hundred and ten clubs, about 1,300 a day at 4,800 Light clubs), so a day at the new-career default (every league Light) costs about 35 seconds while one with most leagues Minimal costs a few. A default of Minimal for the leagues you did not choose would bring the day back; it needs the vacancy fix for out-of-work careers first (a Minimal world has no sackings).
- **Saves of a big world are stored in pieces (a P0 fix):** playtesters reported that new saves failed to load, silently, by every route. The cause was not the career (an out-of-work career saves and loads like any other): a browser refuses one IndexedDB value over about 127 MiB ("The serialized keys and/or value are too large", Chrome), a save of the current world is about 130 MB packed (accented text counts two bytes a character), and the write had no handler for an aborted transaction, so it never finished and never said so, while the slot's small summary had already been written: the title screen listed a save with nothing in it, and loading it did nothing. A long value is now kept as chunks of 24 MB at most with a small manifest, in one transaction (stored whole or not at all, `IDB.put`, `IDB.get`, `Sv.splitValue`); an aborted transaction fails with its reason; the slot's summary is written only after the save is stored (a failed write no longer leaves a slot that lists a save with nothing in it); a save error says why and points to Export save, and a load error shows the real reason. Checked in the browser: an out-of-work career saves (11 chunks), reloads and opens, and a second save over it leaves the same keys. Not done from the report: the sim iteration cap with a visible error and chunking, the save manager, atomic export, the national-fixture stops, vacancies without managers and the job board.
- **A prepared world: a new career starts in seconds:** `tools/buildworld.mjs` (`npm run build:world`, run by the deploy and by `cap:sync` after the build) makes the world once with a fixed seed (every league in the Light tier, the 30 seasons of history and the clubs' legends included), packs it and gzips it as `data/world.touchline` (26 MB; 129 MB packed, 4,860 clubs, 107,361 players) with `data/world.json` naming the simulation build it fits. A new career fetches it (`UI.loadPreparedWorld`), takes the leagues you chose in the new-career screen (`W.fitPreparedWorld`), draws each young player's hidden potential again around what the world gave him (the clubs, squads and history are the same for every save; the futures are not), and fills up the squads of the leagues you run in full (`W.topUpPreparedSquads`, 502 players for a typical start). In the browser a career is ready in 9 seconds, against minutes for a generated world. The new-career screen has a World choice: the shared world (the default) or a random one; the game also makes a world the old way when the file is missing or belongs to another build (a game run from its sources, a failed download), and when a database is chosen. The service worker leaves the file to the browser and the smoke test checks the file belongs to the build. Not done: the long checks (names, history, balance) run once on the shipped world, a lighter file for phones (the world holds 1.3 GB of heap once loaded), and a day of play still costs about 100 CPU seconds at this size with every league Light, in the prepared and the generated world alike.
- **A new world builds six times faster:** a CPU profile of `W.newWorld` (4,860 clubs, 82,661 players) showed 75% of its time in three "is my cache still valid?" checks run for every player made: `W.clubForeign` rebuilt an array of every club to find its league's mean (50%), `W.squad` rebuilt its index of every player after each club's squad (13%), and `W.assignYouth` counted every club (12%). The clubs of each league and the club count are now kept as clubs are made (`W.clubMade`, called by `W.mkClub` and the overseas clubs), and a club's new players are added to the squad index when it was complete. The same seed makes the same world (a hash of the whole packed world matches before and after: 96.0 MB), and a build takes 119 CPU seconds instead of 764. This speeds every test, the gate, the editor and databases, and a new career from the random generator.
- **The army's club says what it is:** Bujeong Garrison's signing policy is `military-service`, so its club picker line, club card and scouting note read "Only military-service players" and "A military-service club: it signs only players of military-service heritage, like Athletic Club". They now say "Conscripts only", "Conscripts on national service" and that its squad is conscripts on loan from their own clubs; other policy clubs (the Basque ones) read as before (`clubguide.js`, `ui-screens.js`, `ui-alpha.js`, `ui-extra.js`).
- **Load a save that is gone says so:** the title screen lists a slot from a small summary in `localStorage`, while the save itself is in IndexedDB; if the save was cleared or its write ran out of room, Continue and Load a save did nothing at all. They now say "Slot N has no save data on this device" (and an error while the game starts a save that did open is shown instead of swallowed) (`UI.load`, `UI.acts.continue`).
- **Real competition formats, sheets 03 to 19:** (1) Domestic cups follow the sheet's legs (24 cups: two-legged semi-finals in Spain, Belgium, Bulgaria, Denmark and Slovenia, whole two-legged cups in Canada and Colombia, two-legged quarter-finals and semi-finals elsewhere), and Argentina and Brazil go straight to penalties, France has extra time only from the round of 32 (`etFrom`, `play.extraTime`). (2) A cup winner without a continental place takes the one his cup carries (`D.CUP_PLACE`, 98 cups), replacing his nation's lowest club in that cup. (3) Continental finals: single matches in the North American and African Champions Cups, two-legged in the Southeast Asian, Gulf and Central American Cups; the Libertadores and South American Shield play extra time in the final only (`etFinalOnly`), as does the Copa América. (4) Guests: the Gold Cup invites an Asian nation; guest selection works for any tournament; the COSAFA, CECAFA and CAFA invitationals take one outsider. Not done, listed in `docs/REAL_FORMATS.md`: the 36-club league phase, group-stage cups, replays (none needed), regional tournament sizes, the fourth international window and 2030's six hosts.
- **Real competition formats, second pass (sheets 03, 05, 06, 17 to 19):** four international windows of 4 + 2 + 2 + 2 matchdays, qualifying groups home and away, the six 2030 World Championship hosts qualifying automatically, group stages for eleven domestic cups and late entry for the top divisions of six, the Gulf, Southeast Asian and Central Asian nations cups drawn from their real members. What the engine does not yet allow (the 36-club league phase, staged confederation qualifying, bigger regional nations cups) is listed in [REAL_FORMATS.md](REAL_FORMATS.md).
- **Real competition formats, third pass (sheet 04):** the European Champions Cup, Shield and Trophy play the 36-club league phase (eight matches each against different opponents, a knockout play-off for ninth to 24th, a round of 16), with more places for each league and qualifying rounds that fill the Champions Cup, and a calendar of eight league-phase matchdays, two play-off days and two round-of-16 days (`D.CC_AFTER`). Monaco stays without a nation, national team or confederation place: it is not a FIFA member, so that is intentional. See [REAL_FORMATS.md](REAL_FORMATS.md).

- **Lower-league stars, part 5: the season digest:** each summer two items go to the news: Late bloomers of the year (the four best players who broke out, among the clubs the game simulates in full) and The step up (the four best who settled after a move to a much bigger club and the four best who struggled) (`Sea.lateDigest`, called from the rollover after the player loop). - **Lower-league stars, part 4: loans for late bloomers and finds:** AI clubs' loan-out list now also takes players of 23 to 27 with at least four points of potential still to come who are either Late Bloomers or hidden gems and are not getting games (`T.lateLoan`, `T.aiLoans`), exempt from the usual 50% skip for senior surplus, and about four loan draws in ten look at that group first. The usual gates still apply (the parent keeps enough bodies and the borrower is a smaller club where he would start), so it is a small extra stream, not a flood. Checked only on a hand-built test world, where the parent clubs' squads were below target and nothing loaned out, so the rate in a real season is not measured.

- **Lower-league stars, part 3: sell-on clauses, finds in the news and free-agent journeymen:** (1) When an AI club sells a hidden gem (`Sea.isGem`) to a club well above it (ten or more reputation), six times in ten the deal carries a 10, 15 or 20% sell-on clause, so the small club shares in his next fee (`T.execute`; it uses the existing `p.sellOn` machinery, so it is paid when he moves again). Your own deals are not changed. (2) Such a move now makes the news as a find from the lower leagues, even when the fee is small (`St.transfer`). (3) Free-agent journeymen are 30% more likely to be picked up by a club below 65 reputation (`T.aiWindow`). Not done: the real-figure calibration, and a change to how AI clubs use loans.

- **Lower-league stars, part 2: journeymen, an Overlooked scouting focus and career lines:** (1) A Journeyman trait, given at the summer rollover to a player of 27 or over who has signed for seven or more different clubs (loans do not count; about 2% of veterans): his permanent deals run one or two years, and he settles into a step up more often (`Sea.permClubs`, `Sea.lateYear`, `T.execute`); a news item when he is yours. (2) A new Overlooked focus in a scouting assignment: the scout favours players clearly better than their club's level and barely known, and a sharper scout (judgement) picks them out more often (`Sc.tick`, `Sea.isGem`). (3) The player's life story adds a line for a late bloomer (the year he came good and his age) and for a journeyman (his clubs). Not done: AI free-agent and loan retuning, sell-on clauses for finds, career stories on the news feed beyond the breakout item, and the real-figure calibration.

- **Lower-league stars, part 1: visibility, breakouts and the step up:** (1) Every player has a hidden visibility (0 to 100): how likely he is to be noticed, apart from how good he is. It starts from his club's standing, his caps and honours; playing above his club's level lifts it a few points a year, and it falls only slowly, so a good player at a small club starts out unseen (`Sea.visTarget`, `Sea.vis`). An AI club's shopping now only considers players its scouts have noticed (a free agent is always on the list; a big club's network reaches wider; the same nation helps), so hidden gems (`Sea.isGem`: at least five above his club's level, 28 or under, visibility under 45) are found late, and move about two and a half times as often as the rest once found (`Sea.noticed`, in `T.aiWindow`). (2) Breakouts: once a season a player of 20 to 28 who has played (12 games, or 8 for a keeper) may break out, with 18% for a Late Bloomer (1.2% for anyone else): two and a half to five and a half ability points at once, his potential raised to fit, his visibility up twelve, and a news item (`Sea.lateYear`, `p.breakout`). (3) The step up: a permanent signing by a club whose level is eight or more above where he was playing is judged at the end of the season. Professionalism, temperament and big-game nerve, age, the gap and his games decide whether he settles (a point or two of growth) or struggles (a point or two lost, lower morale, and a news item if he is yours) (`T.execute`, `p.stepUp`). Not done: the journeyman profile, AI free-agent and loan retuning, the hidden-gems scouting filter and sell-on clauses, career summaries, the real-figure calibration, and an on-pitch penalty during the first season of a step up (it is judged at the end of the season).

- **Youth leftovers: agents, cup ties, the B-team rest rule, the attrition account and FIFA's categories:** (1) a young player (17 to 21) who has hardly played, with an ambitious agent or a family behind him, now asks where his football is coming from (a desk decision): promise him games (about a third of the matches, judged at the end of the season: kept earns trust, broken costs morale and often a transfer request), put him on the loan list, or tell them to wait (`M.agentPush`, `M.promiseCheck`). (2) Cup-tied: a player who has played in a cup this season for another club cannot play in it for his new one (a transfer, or a loanee back from the borrower), though his other competitions stay open; his row carries a Cup-tied tag (`p.cupApps`, `W.pickXI`). (3) A first-team player who played a senior match in the last three days cannot be sent down to the B team (`Y.canSendDown`). (4) The academy report adds the account since you took over (how many the academy made, how many you let go, how many have played senior football) and a Let go too soon group for released youngsters who have grown into players your first team would want. (5) Training compensation now follows FIFA's categories of club: Category I, II, III and IV by a club's standing at 90,000, 60,000, 30,000 and 10,000 a year, his years from twelve to fifteen at the Category IV rate, from sixteen to twenty-one at his club's, and nothing when the signing club is Category IV (`T.trainingFee`). The category rates are from secondary sources and not checked against the current FIFA circular, and the national schemes (England's youth tariff, the French, Spanish and Italian indemnities) are still not modelled.

- **Youth eligibility, call-ups and clashes:** the youth sides now have rules, shown under each side's brief on the Academy tab: the Under-18s take players of 18 or under with ten senior games or fewer this season; the Under-21s take players of 21 or under and up to three overage players (22 to 24) on the fringe of the first team (five senior games or fewer), who fill a short side to find their fitness or minutes; a B team can carry only three players of 23 or over (sending one down past that says so). - **Youth stories, the academy report and the graduates tracker:** an academy graduate's debut card now says when he is a local boy and sometimes that his family are in the stand, and his first senior goal (after his debut) has a card of its own. - **Mentors:** pair a youngster of twenty-one or under with an experienced first-team player (26 or over, two youngsters at most each) from the Mentor chip on his Pathways card, which lists the candidates with their personality and what each would do for him. - **Youth: the coach, your own pick, trials and contract talks:** a youth side is picked better by a better coach (your coach's way with the young, or another club's training ground: a poor one is fooled by a good training week, and good coaching is worth a point or two to a side's strength), and you can pick the side yourself for the Under-18s or Under-21s (up to thirteen players, the coach fills the rest, cleared with one tap) (`Y.coachSkill`, `S.user.youthPick`). - **Training compensation and poaching:** a club that signs a young player (22 or under) it did not train, on a free, now pays the club that trained him: his years in the academy, up to seven, at a yearly rate that rises with the training club's standing, never more than a tenth of what the buyer has (`T.trainingFee`, `T.trainingComp`; it applies to every club, yours included, and shows in the feed when you are paid or pay). A bigger club now comes for one of your best academy prospects (fifteen to eighteen), offering the training compensation: you can sell, offer him a professional deal to keep him (his family and agent may still prefer the bigger club, more so for an ambitious player with no first-team football), or turn them down, and an unanswered approach is turned down (`M.poachYouth`). - **The academy is read as a range, with places and a yearly review:** a young player of your own academy is no longer known through and through. ### Steady scoring pass

- Fixed goals per match creeping from ~2.6 to ~3.0 over 10 seasons; now flat (trend −0.01 to 0.00 a season, every season 2.6–2.9).
- Two root causes: players who developed in the simulation barely grew their secondary attributes, so defending eroded as academy products replaced generated players; and new managers picked tactics at random instead of the usual mix, drifting the world toward open shapes.
- Plus tactical equilibrium: if the whole game drifts, the chance rate eases back toward the calibrated level each summer (capped ±15%, never changing differences between teams).
- Academy and generated-player potential retuned so each generation's elite is closer to the last (elite growth down from +0.5 to about +0.3 ability a season).
- Calibration adds goals-trend, elite-trend and player-shape measures

## Open questions and risks

- **Licensing:** club, league and competition names are trademarks, so the game ships fictional leagues, clubs and competitions with fictional players (known real name combinations are blocked). Real names are left to packs people build themselves with the tools, kept out of the repository and the game's site. Decision (round 8): the game ships fictional; the names database and any pack live in a private folder.
- **Tech stack for release:** proposed answer — keep the web engine inside a native wrapper (Capacitor) rather than porting; the game is plain HTML/CSS/JS with no server.
- **Store updates:** JavaScript updates still normally go through store review, so fixes can't be pushed instantly.
- **Performance at scale:** the 30-league world has about 11,300 players; a league day takes ~0.7–1.35 s and pre-season days ~0.15–0.2 s in Node, and saves are ~7 MB with the compact player format. Save size is not a constraint (the target is a downloadable app with native file storage); the 5 MB localStorage fallback only matters for the web prototype in private browsing. Mid-range phones are still untested (content gate).
- **Balance:** after the training-ground cap, calibration is 49/50 in range on a 3-season run (only miss: champion was a top-3 club, 67% against 70–90%; goals per match 2.77–2.81, over-30s' minutes 20–23%) and the 2-season `npm test` passes.
  - The 10-season wonderkid test (348 followed) is 8/8 in range: 23% reach world class, 59% become good professionals, 18% never pass 69, 6% flop outright, median peak age 26.
  - Earlier: calibration was 50–51/51 on two seeds (title dominance, elite growth, injuries, retirement ages and upsets in range; over-30s' share of top-flight minutes ends six seasons at 24–25%, real 17–28, though it still rises from ~19% early on); one seed still shows the elite improving a little too fast.
  - Finances and difficulty still need tuning from playtests.
- **Scope:** the editor and history modes are large; they may need to ship after 1.0.
